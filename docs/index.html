Both Sides of the Issue
Issues About

For grades 6–8, and anyone who wants a fair briefing

Both sides, in plain language.

Many fights get louder when people do not know the facts. This site explains divisive U.S. issues so a middle school student can follow them — and so each side gets its strongest case, fairly told.

Every issue page has five short parts

  1. What the issue is
  2. Why people disagree
  3. The strongest case for each side
  4. Where they might agree
  5. Questions to think about

Twelve starter issues

Social Security and Global Warming are ready to read. The other pages are outlines, clearly marked Coming soon. They do not include statistics.

Ready to read

Social Security

How should the United States help people when they get old, become disabled, or lose a parent or spouse — and how should it pay for that help?

A monthly government payment for retirees, people with disabilities, and some families, and a hard question about how to keep paying for it as the country gets older.

Read the page Coming soon

Energy

How should the United States power homes, travel, and factories — and what should we do about cost, reliability, and pollution?

Oil, gas, coal, nuclear, wind, solar, and water each keep the country running in a different way. This page will lay out the tradeoffs.

Global Warming

How is the Earth’s climate changing, what role do people play, and what should the country do about it?

The measurements of carbon dioxide and temperature, kept separate from the argument over how much harm is coming and what the rules should be.

Read the page Coming soon

Education

How should the United States run and pay for schools, and should families be able to choose a school other than the one assigned to them?

A short page about what schools cost, what students learn, and the argument over school choice. It will not try to swallow every argument about education.

See the outline Coming soon

Taxes

Who should pay taxes in the United States, and what should those taxes be for?

Who pays, what the money funds, and why people use the word “fair” to mean different things.

See the outline Coming soon

National Debt

How much should the U.S. government borrow, and who has to deal with the bill later?

The debt is the money the federal government has borrowed and not yet paid back. This page will explain the size of the argument, not invent a size for the debt.

See the outline Coming soon

Healthcare

Who should pay for health care in the United States, and how should that care be organized?

A narrow starting question: who pays the bill for health care, and what happens when the answer is a family, an employer, an insurer, or the government.

See the outline Coming soon

Immigration

Who should be allowed to come live and work in the United States, and how should the rules be enforced?

The rules for coming to the country, and the argument over how those rules should work. This page will stay with the big picture and will not lead with contested claims about voting.

See the outline Coming soon

Free Speech

What speech should the law protect, and where do people disagree about the limits?

A civics page about the First Amendment: what the government may not punish, and the arguments over speech that is cruel, false, or dangerous.

See the outline Coming soon

Gun Control

What should the laws say about who may have a gun?

A calm page about gun laws. It will include strong evidence used by both sides, stay age-appropriate, and skip graphic stories.

See the outline Coming soon

Income, Wealth, and Poverty

Why do some people have much more than others, and what should the country do about poverty?

Living standards, what “poor” means in the statistics, and the argument over inequality. Definitions first, numbers only after they are checked.

Crime

How should we understand crime in the United States, and what should the justice system do about it?

A big-picture page about crime rates and the justice system. It will not be a list of sensational cases.
